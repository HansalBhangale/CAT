## 0. Read this first — assumptions, the one big insight, and a reality check

### Assumptions I made (adjust if wrong)
- You can hit 4 hours *most* days, with a little more flex on weekends (useful for mock days).
- You have a working math/English base already (consistent with "QA, DI, RC are strengths").
- **VA (Verbal Ability — para jumbles, para summary, odd-one-out, para completion) was not listed**, so I'm treating it as **"maintain + monitor"**, not a known strength. If your VA is shaky, tell me and I'll reweight VARC time.
- You're starting roughly from scratch on a structured routine (no current mock baseline given). **Take one diagnostic mock in Week 1** to set your baseline — every later number is relative to that.

### The single most important thing in this entire plan
**LR is not a section. It lives *inside* DILR, right next to your DI strength.**

CAT has three sections: VARC (24 Q), **DILR (22 Q)**, QA (22 Q). Your strength (DI) and your weakness (LR) share *one* 40-minute combined section. That means:
- You cannot "play to your strengths and hide your weakness" — your weakness is bolted onto your strength.
- **DILR is the highest-leverage area in your entire prep.** A strong DI base + a fixed LR turns your weakest-feeling section into a *weapon*, and DILR is the section that decides toppers because it's hard for everyone.
- Roughly **40–50% of your discretionary study energy for the first 3 months should go at DILR, weighted toward LR.** This is the plan's backbone.

### Reality check on 99.99 (read once, then stop worrying about it)
~2.58 lakh candidates appeared for CAT 2025. At that scale, **99.99%ile ≈ the top ~26 candidates** in the country — single/low-double-digit All-India Rank territory. No study plan can *guarantee* that; it needs elite aptitude **plus** flawless exam-day execution **plus** favourable slot difficulty/normalization.

So here's the honest framing this plan uses:
- **Engineer for 99.9+** (itself top ~250 nationally). That is genuinely achievable with the work below.
- **Treat 99.99 as the north star**, not the KPI. Chase the *process*; the percentile is the byproduct.
- **Mock %iles ≠ real CAT %iles.** Different test-taker pools, different normalization. Track the *trend* of your mocks, never obsess over a single absolute number.

## 1. The exam, so the targets make sense

| Section | Questions | Marks | Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VARC | 24 | 72 | 40 min | ~16 RC + ~8 VA. RC dominates. |
| DILR | 22 | 66 | 40 min | Usually ~4–5 sets (mix of 4-Q and 5-Q sets). **Your battleground.** |
| QA | 22 | 66 | 40 min | Arithmetic-heavy, then Algebra, Geometry, Number System, Modern Math. |
| **Total** | **68** | **204** | **120 min** | Sections in fixed order VARC → DILR → QA. **No going back** once a section's 40 min ends. |

**Marking:** +3 correct, −1 wrong MCQ, **0 for wrong/blank TITA** (so attempt every TITA you have any read on). On-screen calculator provided — so DI is about *strategy and reading*, not arithmetic speed.

### What a ~99.9+ profile tends to look like (directional, not a promise)
Normalization shifts this every year, but as a mental model of *clean* attempts + high accuracy:
- **QA:** attempt ~16–20 of 22, accuracy ~85%+. (Strong area → push for near-perfect on what you attempt.)
- **VARC:** attempt ~18–22, accuracy ~80%+. RC accuracy is the lever.
- **DILR:** solve **3–4 sets cleanly (≈12–16 Q)** at high accuracy. Because the section is brutal for most, *clean* selection here is what separates 99.9 from 95.

Don't memorize these numbers. The controllables are: **set/question selection, accuracy, and not-bleeding-marks.** Those are what we train.

## 5. Weak-area strategy — Logical Reasoning (the centerpiece)

LR feels hard for three fixable reasons, not because you "can't do it":
1. **Set selection** — picking the wrong sets and grinding them is the #1 score-killer. This is a *meta-skill*, trainable separately from solving.
2. **Representation** — most LR sets collapse the moment you draw the right grid/table/diagram. Wrong representation = pain.
3. **Pattern unfamiliarity** — after ~150–200 sets, almost every CAT LR set feels like a variant of something you've seen.

### The four-stage LR build (maps onto the phases)

**Stage A — Learn the menu (Jun, Wk 1–5).** One set-type at a time, **untimed.** For each type build a one-page playbook entry: *what it looks like → the representation to draw → the 2–3 standard traps.* Goal: ~70%+ accuracy untimed, all types covered.

**Stage B — Single-set timing (Jul, Wk 6–9).** Now time individual sets. Log **time-per-set** and **accuracy by type.** Start at ~12–15 min/set, drive toward 10–12. Identify your "comfort types" vs "avoid types."

**Stage C — Set selection under section pressure (Aug–Sep).** Practice with **6 sets in 40 min, pick 4.** The drill is: spend the first ~2–3 min *reading all sets and choosing*, then attack easiest-first. **Train the abandon reflex** — if a chosen set isn't cracking in ~3–4 min past your estimate, leave it. Sunk cost is the enemy.

**Stage D — Integration (Oct–Nov).** DILR full-section reps where DI + LR compete for the same 40 min. By here LR should no longer be the limiter — it should be a *source* of clean sets.

### Daily LR ritual
- **2–3 sets/day, every day.** Consistency beats binge.
- **Review every set, even the ones you got right** — note the *optimal* path, not just a correct one.
- **LR error log** (separate tab): for each miss, tag the *cause* —
  `misread condition` · `wrong representation` · `calc slip` · `ran out of time` · `bad set selection`.
  Patterns in this column tell you exactly what to drill.

### LR progression milestones
| By end of | LR state |
|---|---|
| **July** | All set-types known; timed single sets at ~10–12 min, ~75% accuracy |
| **September** | In a 40-min DILR section, cleanly solve **2–3 LR sets**; set-selection instinct forming |
| **October** | **3 sets** at reasonable speed, ~80%+ accuracy; DILR mock %ile climbing past 90 → 95 |
| **November** | LR is *not* your limiter; DILR is a strength on the merits |

## 7. Mock & sectional schedule

| Month | Full mocks | Plus |
|---|---|---|
| **June** | 1 diagnostic (Wk 1 only) | Topic tests as you finish each topic |
| **July** | 0–1 | Sectional tests (single-section, 40 min) once set-types are learned |
| **August** | ~4 (1/wk, Sundays) | 2 sectionals/wk |
| **September** | ~6 (ramp to 1–2/wk) | Sectionals on off-days |
| **October** | ~10–12 (2–3/wk) | Targeted set drills |
| **November** | ~6–8 early, then **taper** | Error-log redo, light revision |

**Total ≈ 28–34 full mocks** + many sectionals/topic tests. That's plenty *if* analyzed. More mocks with shallow analysis is worse than fewer with deep analysis.

### The mock analysis protocol (do this every single time)
1. **Don't peek at the score first.** Open the *solutions/your responses* before the percentile.
2. **Re-solve, untimed, everything you got wrong or guessed** — until you can do it cleanly yourself.
3. **Categorize every error:** concept gap · silly slip · time pressure · **bad selection** (attempted a question/set you should've skipped) · misread.
4. **Selection audit (most important for you):** for DILR especially — *which sets did you do, which should you have done?* Replay the choosing decision.
5. **Log it.** Every error → error log with cause tag. Every "should've skipped" → selection notes.
6. **One-line takeaway per section:** "Next mock I will ____." Carry it into the next attempt.

Spending less than ~2× the mock duration on analysis = wasting the mock.

## 8. Revision system

- **Daily (30 min slot):** log today's errors + spaced-review ~10 old errors.
- **Weekly (the lighter day):** sweep the week's error log, redo the still-shaky ones, update playbooks.
- **Living documents** (build from Day 1, revise weekly):
  - **QA formula/shortcut sheet** (1–2 pages).
  - **LR set-type playbook** (one entry per type: look → representation → traps).
  - **VA rules sheet** (jumble/summary/odd-one-out heuristics).
  - **Master error log** (the single highest-ROI artifact you'll build).
- **Spaced repetition for errors:** review a logged error at ~day 1, day 3, day 7, day 21. An error you've truly fixed graduates out of the log.
- **The final-month rule:** in November you mostly revise *your own materials* — formula sheet, playbook, error log. **No new topics after ~mid-October.**

## 12. Burnout prevention (the part that decides whether you finish)

Six months is a marathon. The aspirants who hit 99.9+ aren't the ones who studied hardest in week 3 — they're the ones still sharp and uninjured in week 26.

**Sustainability rules**
- **Sleep 7–8 hours, non-negotiable.** Sleep is when memory consolidates and pattern-recognition (your LR superpower) is built. Trading sleep for an extra study hour is a *net loss*.
- **One lighter day per week** — half the load or active recovery. Protects against accumulation.
- **Move 3–4×/week** — even 20–30 min walks. Aerobic exercise measurably helps focus and mood.
- **Protect a little life** — keep one hobby/social thing weekly. Total monasticism breaks people by month 3.

**The "Minimum Viable Day"** (for low-energy/sick/overwhelmed days)
Don't break the streak and don't spiral into guilt. On a bad day, do just **1 hour: 2 LR sets + a 15-min error-log review.** That's a complete, honest day. The streak survives; tomorrow you're back to 4.

**Deload weeks**
Every ~6–7 hard weeks, take a lighter week (~50–60% load). You'll come back *faster*, not slower. Counterintuitive, real.

**Warning signs to take seriously**
Dread before sitting down · scores flat-or-falling despite effort · sleep getting worse · irritability · can't focus for even 20 min. **→ Take 2–3 real days off.** A short, deliberate break beats weeks of grinding while fried.

**Don't compare.** Someone online "did 8 hours and 50 mocks." Ignore it. Your only competitor is last week's you. Track *your* trend.

**The taper (final 7–10 days) is a burnout-prevention tool too.** Winding down isn't slacking — a rested, calm brain on 29 November outperforms an exhausted, anxious one that "studied more." No new topics, light revision, normal sleep, and arrive at the exam *fresh*.
